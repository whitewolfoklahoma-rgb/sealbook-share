How SealBook prices a job

Sealer is computed, not guessed. Enter the measured square footage and SealBook works out the gallons from your coverage rate (sq ft per gallon per coat, in Settings), then costs the sealer and sand additive at your per-gallon prices. Two coats and premium sealer change the material bill automatically.

Crack fill is priced and costed by the linear foot. Striping is priced per stall / ADA stall / curb foot, and the paint usage is computed from the line length those stalls lay down.

Labor comes from your production rates: sealing sq ft per crew-hour (a second coat takes 70% longer than one, not double — the lot is already prepped and edged), crack-fill feet per hour, striping feet per hour, plus your setup/mobilization time, times crew size times your labor rate.

Good / Better / Best: Good = 1 coat standard sealer. Better = 2 coats standard. Best = 2 coats premium sealer. Crack fill, striping, and extras are identical on all three — only the sealing changes, which is the honest way to present the choice.

Profit = price (before tax) minus material, labor, and your other job costs. If a job's margin falls under 15%, SealBook flags it so you see it before you book the work, not after.

What SealBook does not do

It does not measure lots from satellite photos — measure the lot (wheel, tape, or the customer's plat) and enter the square footage. It has no account, no sync, and no online booking: it is one file, on your device, that works in a parking lot with no signal. That is the point.

Getting your numbers right

The defaults are national planning numbers (85 sq ft/gal/coat coverage, $3.10/gal bulk standard sealer, $0.17/sq ft one-coat / $0.27 two-coat). Spend ten minutes in Settings making them yours: your actual sealer price per gallon, your coverage on your lots, your crew's real production. Then check the first few jobs' actual profit in the Job Book and tune.
